Treatment Algorithm: Massive Irreparable Rotator Cuff Tears Without Pseudoparalysis
Evidence-Based Flowsheet | Active Forward Elevation >90° | No Glenohumeral Arthritis (unless noted) | Literature 2020-2025
Confirmed Massive Irreparable Rotator Cuff Tear
Active Forward Elevation >90°  |  No Pseudoparalysis  |  Failed Conservative Management
Glenohumeral Arthritis (Hamada ≥3)?
↓ Yes
Reverse Total Shoulder
Arthroplasty (rTSA)
Standard indication with arthritis
↓ No Arthritis
Patient Age & Functional Demand
Age >70 or Low Demand
Goal: Pain relief, simple recovery
1st Line
Arthroscopic Debridement + Biceps Tenotomy
Subacromial decompression, tuberoplasty
Reliable pain relief, minimal morbidity
If insufficient relief ↓
2nd Line
Subacromial Balloon Spacer (InSpace)
± Partial repair if tissue available
CS 34→68, ASES 35→83 at 2yr
5-year data shows sustained benefit
Salvage: rTSA
If joint-preserving options fail
Any Age | Partially Reducible Tissue
Goal: Maximize repair, preserve joint
1st Line
Partial Repair +/- Bioinductive Augmentation
Margin convergence, medialized footprint
REGENETEN patch: 68% retear reduction (RCT)
Biceps tenotomy/tenodesis as adjunct
If not reparable ↓
2nd Line
Superior Capsular Reconstruction
Dermal allograft or LHB autograft
87-90% pseudoparalysis reversal
Technically demanding; declining use
Salvage: rTSA or Tendon Transfer
Based on age, demand, tear pattern
Age <65 or High Demand
Goal: Restore biomechanics, long-term function
1st Line: Posterosuperior Tear
Lower Trapezius Transfer (preferred)
Better ER vector than lat dorsi (biomechanical)
ASES improves 22-49 pts; requires allograft
Alt: Latissimus Dorsi Transfer (established)
1st Line: Anterosuperior Tear
Pectoralis Major Transfer
For irreparable subscapularis component
Requires intact posterior cuff
Consider
Partial Repair + Tendon Transfer
Combined approach for maximum restoration
Repair what is reducible + transfer for remainder
Salvage: rTSA (caution if age <60)
Risk of DECREASED FE if preop >90°
High complication rate in young (26-39%)
Key Principle: If active FE >90° and no arthritis, exhaust joint-preserving options before rTSA. rTSA may DECREASE elevation in this subgroup.
Sources: Bi et al. AJSM 2024 (network meta-analysis), Kunze et al. AJSM 2024 (balloon spacer), REGENETEN RCT 2024, LTT systematic review 2025, Sheth & Shah AJSM 2023
